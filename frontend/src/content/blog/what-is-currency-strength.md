## What Does "Currency Strength" Mean?

Currency strength is a numerical score that measures **how a single currency is performing relative to all other major currencies** at any given moment. Rather than looking at just one pair (like EUR/USD), currency strength analyzes a currency across **all its pairs simultaneously** to give you a holistic view.

Think of it like a leaderboard. Instead of comparing two football teams head-to-head, you compare every team's performance across all their matches to see who is truly the best — and who is struggling.

For forex traders, this leaderboard is incredibly powerful. It reveals the **strongest and weakest currencies** in real time, helping you identify the most promising trade setups before they become obvious on a single chart.

## How Is Currency Strength Calculated?

The calculation involves three key steps:

### 1. Collect Price Data Across All Pairs

For each of the 8 major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, CAD, AUD, NZD), the system tracks every possible pair. That means the **28 unique forex pairs** formed by these 8 currencies are continuously monitored.

### 2. Measure Percentage Change

For each pair, the system calculates the **percentage change** over a specific timeframe (5 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours, 24 hours, etc.). This shows how much each currency has gained or lost against its counterpart.

### 3. Aggregate Into a Single Score

The individual pair performances are aggregated into a **single score per currency**, typically on a 0-100 scale. A higher score means the currency is strengthening across the board; a lower score means it's weakening.

Some advanced systems (like the [LiveForexStrength meter](/)) also apply **liquidity weighting**, giving more importance to highly traded pairs like EUR/USD than to less liquid ones like NZD/CHF.

## Why Currency Strength Matters for Trading

### Find High-Probability Trades

The golden rule of currency strength trading is simple: **buy the strongest, sell the weakest**. When the AUD scores 85 and the JPY scores 15, the AUD/JPY pair has a strong directional bias. You're stacking the odds in your favor because both currencies are moving in your direction.

### Avoid Choppy Pairs

If both EUR and GBP are scoring around 50, the EUR/GBP pair is likely **ranging and choppy**. No clear winner, no clear trend. Currency strength saves you from entering low-conviction trades.

### Confirm Your Analysis

Already found a trade setup on a chart? Cross-reference it with currency strength. If your technical analysis says "buy GBP/USD" but the strength meter shows GBP weakening and USD strengthening, that's a major red flag.

### Spot Divergences Early

Sometimes a currency starts strengthening before it shows on popular pairs. The strength meter catches this early because it aggregates **all pairs**, not just the one you're watching.

## What Makes a Currency Strong or Weak?

Several factors drive currency strength:

### Interest Rates
Central bank policy is the single biggest driver. Higher rates attract foreign investment, strengthening the currency. The USD typically strengthens when the Federal Reserve raises rates.

### Economic Data
GDP growth, employment numbers, inflation data, and trade balances all influence strength. Strong economic data = strong currency.

### Risk Sentiment
In times of market fear, "safe haven" currencies like the USD, JPY, and CHF tend to strengthen. In risk-on environments, commodity currencies like AUD, NZD, and CAD outperform.

### Commodity Prices
AUD is closely tied to iron ore and gold. CAD follows oil prices. NZD tracks dairy exports. When these commodities rally, the linked currencies tend to strengthen.
