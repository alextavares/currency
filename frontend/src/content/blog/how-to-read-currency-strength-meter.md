## What Is a Currency Strength Meter?

A currency strength meter is a visual tool that displays the **relative strength of major currencies** on a scale, usually from 0 to 100. It takes data from multiple forex pairs and condenses it into **one easy-to-read score per currency**.

Instead of flipping between 28 different charts, you get a single dashboard showing exactly which currencies are dominating and which are struggling — in real time.

## Understanding the Score Scale

On the [LiveForexStrength meter](/), each currency receives a score between 0 and 100:

| Score Range | Label | Meaning |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| **75–100** | Strong | Currency gaining significantly across most pairs |
| **55–74** | Slightly Strong | Mild bullish bias |
| **45–54** | Neutral | No clear direction; balanced |
| **25–44** | Slightly Weak | Mild bearish bias |
| **0–24** | Weak | Currency losing significantly across most pairs |

The key insight: **extreme scores (above 80 or below 20) represent the highest-conviction opportunities**. When a currency is at 90 while another sits at 10, there is a powerful directional flow that you can ride.

## Step 1: Identify the Extremes

The first thing to do when opening a strength meter is to look at the **top and bottom of the ranking**. Ask yourself:

- Which currency is the strongest right now?
- Which currency is the weakest?
- How big is the gap between them?

A gap of **40+ points** between the strongest and weakest currencies signals a high-probability trading condition. A small gap (under 15 points) suggests the market is ranging and lacks conviction.

## Step 2: Form the Optimal Pair

Once you've identified the strongest and weakest currencies, combine them into a pair:

**Strongest + Weakest = Highest probability trade**

For example:
- AUD at 82, JPY at 18 → **Buy AUD/JPY**
- USD at 85, NZD at 12 → **Buy USD/NZD** (or sell NZD/USD)

This is the fundamental principle of strength-based trading. You're not guessing — you're trading with the flow of the entire market behind you.

## Step 3: Check Multiple Timeframes

A currency can be strong on the 5-minute chart but weak on the 4-hour chart. That's a conflict. For the best results, look for **alignment across timeframes**:

- **5m + 1h aligned**: Good for day trades
- **1h + 4h aligned**: Great for swing trades
- **4h + 24h aligned**: Strong for position trades

The [LiveForexStrength dashboard](/) shows all timeframes simultaneously, making this cross-check effortless.

When the 5m, 1h, and 4h all agree that AUD is strong, that's a **much more reliable signal** than if only the 5m shows it.

## Step 4: Use the Heatmap for Confirmation

After identifying your trade via the strength meter, switch to the [Forex Heatmap](/heatmap). The heatmap shows the **percentage change of every pair** as a color-coded grid:

- **Dark green**: Strong bullish move
- **Light green**: Mild bullish move
- **Red**: Bearish move
- **Gray**: Flat, no movement

If the strength meter says "Buy AUD/JPY" and the heatmap shows AUD/JPY in dark green, you have **double confirmation**. If the heatmap shows it as flat or red, reconsider the trade.

## Step 5: Plan Your Entry

The strength meter tells you **what to trade**, not **when to enter**. For timing, use your preferred technical analysis:

1. **Support/Resistance**: Wait for a pullback to a key level
2. **Moving Averages**: Enter when price crosses above/below key MAs
3. **Candlestick Patterns**: Look for engulfing patterns, pin bars, etc.
4. **Breakouts**: Enter when the pair breaks a recent high/low with strong strength backing

The beauty of combining strength data with chart analysis is that you already know the **direction** is in your favor. You're just optimizing the entry.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Trading Neutral Currencies
If both currencies in a pair score between 45 and 55, there's no clear bias. Don't force a trade. Wait for divergence.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Timeframe Conflicts
A 5-minute strength reading can reverse quickly. If the 4-hour reading contradicts it, trust the longer timeframe or wait for alignment.

### Mistake 3: Chasing Extreme Reversals
When a currency hits 95, some traders try to short it, expecting a reversal. This is **counter-trend trading** and is risky. Extreme strength usually continues before reversing. Don't fight the trend.

### Mistake 4: Not Checking Correlations
AUD and NZD are highly correlated. If both are strong, trading AUD/NZD won't give you much movement. Instead, pair one of them against a truly weak currency like JPY or USD.
