## Which Currencies Are the Strongest in 2025?

When people ask "what is the strongest currency in the world?", they usually mean the one with the highest exchange rate. But for forex traders, **strength means something different** — it measures how well a currency is performing across all its pairs right now.

A currency can have a high absolute value (like the GBP) but still be weakening. Conversely, a "cheaper" currency (like the AUD) can be the strongest performer on any given day.

In this guide, we rank all 8 major currencies and explain what drives each one. You can check the live rankings anytime on the [LiveForexStrength meter](/).

## The 8 Major Currencies Ranked

### 1. US Dollar (USD) 🇺🇸

The world's reserve currency and the most traded currency globally. The USD accounts for approximately **88% of all forex transactions**.

**What drives USD strength:**
- Federal Reserve interest rate decisions
- Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) and jobs data
- US GDP growth
- Global risk sentiment (safe haven demand)
- US Treasury yields

**Key characteristic**: The USD tends to strengthen during market uncertainty and risk-off periods. When global markets panic, capital flows into USD-denominated assets.

### 2. Euro (EUR) 🇪🇺

The second most traded currency, representing the 20 countries of the Eurozone. EUR/USD is by far the most liquid forex pair in the world.

**What drives EUR strength:**
- European Central Bank (ECB) monetary policy
- Eurozone inflation (CPI) data
- German manufacturing (PMI) data
- Political stability across member states
- Trade balance with the US and China

**Key characteristic**: EUR tends to strengthen when the ECB is hawkish (raising rates or signaling tightening) and when the Eurozone economy outperforms expectations.

[Track EUR strength live →](/currencies/eur)

### 3. British Pound (GBP) 🇬🇧

One of the oldest currencies still in circulation. GBP historically has one of the highest absolute values among major currencies.

**What drives GBP strength:**
- Bank of England (BoE) rate decisions
- UK inflation and employment data
- Housing market data
- Brexit-related developments and trade agreements
- UK retail sales and consumer confidence

**Key characteristic**: GBP is one of the most volatile major currencies. It reacts sharply to BoE decisions and can move 100+ pips in seconds after rate announcements.

[Track GBP strength live →](/currencies/gbp)

### 4. Japanese Yen (JPY) 🇯🇵

The most important Asian currency in forex trading. JPY pairs are among the most popular for carry trades.

**What drives JPY strength:**
- Bank of Japan (BoJ) ultra-loose monetary policy shifts
- Japanese government bond yields
- Global risk sentiment (JPY strengthens when markets fall)
- US-Japan interest rate differential
- Japanese trade balance data

**Key characteristic**: JPY is the ultimate safe haven. When stock markets crash or geopolitical tensions rise, JPY surges as traders unwind carry positions. Watch JPY strength as a fear gauge.

[Track JPY strength live →](/currencies/jpy)

### 5. Swiss Franc (CHF) 🇨🇭

Switzerland's reputation for financial stability makes CHF another classic safe haven currency.

**What drives CHF strength:**
- Swiss National Bank (SNB) policy and interventions
- European political instability (capital flight to Switzerland)
- Global risk sentiment
- Swiss inflation data
- Gold prices (Switzerland has historically large gold reserves)

**Key characteristic**: The SNB is known for surprise interventions, directly buying or selling CHF to control its value. This makes CHF one of the more unpredictable currencies for trend followers.

[Track CHF strength live →](/currencies/chf)

### 6. Canadian Dollar (CAD) 🇨🇦

Known as the "Loonie," CAD is closely tied to commodity prices — especially oil.

**What drives CAD strength:**
- Crude oil prices (Canada is a major oil exporter)
- Bank of Canada rate decisions
- Canadian employment and GDP data
- US economic health (Canada's largest trading partner)
- Housing market data

**Key characteristic**: If you want to trade CAD, watch oil. When WTI crude price rises, CAD almost always strengthens. This commodity correlation makes CAD one of the most predictable currencies.

[Track CAD strength live →](/currencies/cad)

### 7. Australian Dollar (AUD) 🇦🇺

A commodity currency tied to iron ore, gold, and China's economic activity.

**What drives AUD strength:**
- Iron ore and gold prices
- Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) rate decisions
- Chinese economic data (Australia's largest export market)
- Australian employment data
- Risk sentiment (AUD strengthens in risk-on environments)

**Key characteristic**: AUD is a risk-on currency. When global growth is strong and commodity demand is high, AUD outperforms. In recessions or China slowdowns, AUD weakens significantly.

[Track AUD strength live →](/currencies/aud)

### 8. New Zealand Dollar (NZD) 🇳🇿

The "Kiwi" is the smallest economy among the majors but still plays a significant role in forex.

**What drives NZD strength:**
- Dairy prices (New Zealand's top export)
- Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) policy
- Chinese demand for NZ exports
- GlobalDairyTrade (GDT) auction results
- Risk sentiment (similar to AUD)

**Key characteristic**: NZD is highly correlated with AUD. Both tend to move in the same direction, but NZD is more volatile because New Zealand's economy is smaller and more export-dependent.

## How Currency Strength Changes Throughout the Day

Currency strength isn't static — it shifts throughout the 24-hour trading day based on which financial centers are open:

| Session | Hours (UTC) | Most Active Currencies |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| Sydney/Tokyo | 00:00 – 08:00 | AUD, NZD, JPY |
| London | 08:00 – 16:00 | EUR, GBP, CHF |
| New York | 13:00 – 21:00 | USD, CAD |
| London/NY Overlap | 13:00 – 16:00 | **All major currencies** (highest volatility) |

The London/New York overlap is when currency strength shifts are most dramatic and trading opportunities are most abundant.
